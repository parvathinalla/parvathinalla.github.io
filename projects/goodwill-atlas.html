THE PROBLEM / THE WORK

Why this exists

Corporate acquisition disclosures are difficult to analyze consistently. Key financial figures can appear in dense narrative sections, financial tables, and measurement-period adjustments. The central design question was: how can a research interface show not just an answer, but the source passage that supports it?

How I approached it

I built an independently developed extension to my UF financial NLP research area. The prototype uses a document-processing pipeline with normalized SEC filing HTML, heading-aware sections, stable evidence chunks, retrieval, extraction, and structured research output. It keeps unsupported fields empty rather than inventing values.

Under the hood

  • Ingestion: bounded SEC requests, explicit user-agent configuration, validation, and retry/backoff.
  • Retrieval: an offline lexical baseline with an optional OpenAI embedding + FAISS pathway.
  • Verification: each extracted fact is tied to a source passage and unresolved values are flagged for review.
  • Product experience: a research dashboard, individual filing dossiers, searchable evidence, and typed REST APIs.
Research and evaluation integrity: The included evaluation is based on fictional, synthetic filings. It is not an audited accuracy claim on real SEC disclosures. Live SEC ingestion requires configuration and independent validation. Goodwill Atlas is an independent extension, not a claimed faculty deliverable.
