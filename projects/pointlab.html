THE PROBLEM / THE WORK

Why this exists

Research notebooks make excellent experiments, but often make it hard for another engineer to explore what changed, why it changed, or whether two sampling choices behave differently. PointLab turns core geometric operations into something visible and comparable.

How I approached it

The open-source project packages three original research notebooks with a browser-based point-cloud visualization environment. The interactive demo focuses on geometric concepts—farthest-point sampling (FPS), k-nearest neighbors (kNN), patch exploration, and masking controls—rather than pretending to be a trained autonomous-driving predictor.

Under the hood

  • Interactive point-cloud viewing with orbital camera controls, procedural demo scenes, and local point-cloud uploads.
  • Adjustable FPS patch-center count, k-neighborhood size, masking ratio, and geometry recomputation.
  • Repeatable FPS-vs-random spatial-coverage comparison reporting mean, 95th percentile, and worst-case center distance.
  • Documentation preserves experimental provenance, research notebooks, tests, and known limitations.
Research and evaluation integrity: Displayed procedural point clouds and sampling benchmarks are demonstrations, not learned Point-BERT tokens or validated nuScenes prediction results. The public GitHub repository is live at v0.2.0 with the interactive workbench, test suite, and research notebooks.
